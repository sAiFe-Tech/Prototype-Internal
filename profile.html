01 — THE PROBLEM

Every family has this problem. Most just haven't admitted it yet.

Think about your own household for a moment. Do you know exactly where your home insurance policy is? Could your partner find the children's passport numbers without calling you? If something happened to you tomorrow, would the people you love be able to access everything they needed — quickly, calmly, without panic?

For most families the honest answer is no. Not because they're disorganised people — but because life is complex, fast-moving, and relentless. Think about the last time someone asked for the Netflix password. Or the WiFi code. Or which email address the Amazon account is under. Or whether you're still paying for that gym membership nobody uses. These are not crises — but they are the daily texture of a household that isn't organised. And underneath those small frustrations sit the genuinely important things: the insurance policy, the mortgage documents, the will, the medical records.

The average household is actively paying for between eight and twelve subscription services at any one time. Most families cannot name them all. Many are paying for services that haven't been used in months — wasting over £600 a year on forgotten or duplicate subscriptions. Every year the volume grows. Every year the anxiety and waste grow with it.

02 — WHY EVERY PREVIOUS SOLUTION HAS FAILED

The graveyard of document storage apps is long. Here is why.

This is not a new problem. Dozens of companies have attempted to solve it — from cloud storage giants like Google Drive and Dropbox, to dedicated tools like Evernote, Everplans, and Legacy Locker. Most are still running. None have truly solved it.

The reason is always the same: they built filing cabinets, not companions. A filing cabinet is passive. You put things in, you take things out. It does not care whether you come back tomorrow or in three years. It has no reason to earn your daily attention. And so people set it up with good intentions, never quite finish, and quietly drift away.

The engagement problem kills document storage products. Without a reason to return every day, the vault becomes another drawer — digital instead of physical, but just as neglected. And critically, these tools do nothing about the sprawling web of subscriptions, accounts, and credentials that a modern household accumulates.

MySAiFe was designed from day one to solve the engagement problem first. The vault is the foundation. The Agent is the reason you come back.

The filing cabinet analogy only works if someone is motivated to keep it updated. MySAiFe inverts the model — the system does the work, surfaces what matters, and gives users a living, breathing picture of their household that improves every time they interact with it. The more they put in, the more value they get out. That flywheel is what every previous product missed.
